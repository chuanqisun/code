Hold That Thought:A Somatic-Symbolic-Semantic Architecture for Shaping Ideas in Real Time

Somatic: The body: hands, voice, and gesture
Symbolic: Handles that code understands
Semantic: Meaning that models understand
Shaping Ideas in Real Time: A closed loop with more agency, creativity, and joy

“Get a handle on it” is an idiom for a reason. A handle is the oldest interface for gripping something slippery.

The model supplies meaning, a symbolic layer supplies the handle, and the speed keeps it a conversation with material.

Click 1: Somatic, the body (the person lights up). Click 2: Symbolic, handles that code understands (the handles light up). Click 3: Semantic, meaning that models understand (the network lights up). Click 4: Shaping ideas in real time, a closed loop with more agency, creativity, and joy (the whole picture).

State the hypothesis in one sentence: route the body through symbols to meaning, close every loop in real time, and people keep more agency, create more, and feel more joy than in turn-based chat.

The S3 Architecture

Three nodes. Somatic: the body. Symbolic: what code interprets. Semantic: what a model interprets.

Click 1: Direct Manipulation connects body and symbol. Sketchpad, the mouse, Tangible Bits. Reliable, but only for things that already have a symbolic form.

Click 2: Interface Agents connect body and meaning directly: vision, speech, chat. They jump over the symbolic node. That is the 1997 Shneiderman–Maes debate in one picture: expressive, but nothing to hold and hard to predict.

Click 3: Live Interpretation, symbolic ↔ semantic: “structured reasons, reasoned structures.” Parametric prompts carry symbols into a model; detection, decisions, and code carry meaning back out as structure. The loop on the right is the model computing on its own.

Click 4: two recent papers, one per edge. Declarative Dragging (Horowitz, Blinn, Heer, UIST ’26) makes drag-and-drop model-driven. DirectGPT (Masson et al., CHI ’24) brings direct manipulation to LLM output, but still turn by turn.

Click 5: Hold That Thought routes the body through the symbol to meaning and back, and closes the loop in real time. The agents fade; the loop runs through the middle.

The 3rd Interface

Step back from S3 to the three parties: human, machine, AI.

Click 1: human ↔ machine. HCI, the first interface. Sixty years of it, from Sketchpad to the touchscreen.

Click 2: human ↔ AI. HAI, the second interface. Chat, voice, agents, the one everyone is building now.

Click 3: AI ↔ machine. The third interface has no name yet. In S3 terms it is interpretation: where meaning meets code. That is the interface this thesis designs, because it decides whether the human can still hold on.

Sculpt: Sable. Shape a terrain by hand and hear the timbre change. Be honest that it is a web sketch today.

Puck Grammar is a tangible neuro-symbolic language. A camera tracks the pucks, and the arrangement is the program: position, proximity, and rotation map to verbs such as steer, blend, bind, and lock.

This is the computer-vision grammar running live. Open questions: do the verbs compose and abstract, and can people learn the notation?

Actuated Puck Table
Pucks are just magnets.
Anything can become a handle.

Click 1: credit. This is Shuji Hosokawa’s work.

The runtime: a PCB coil array moves passive magnetic pucks. Scalable, modular, silent, 2.5D. Any object with a magnet becomes a handle.

This closes the third loop of S3: the AI proposes by moving a handle, or resists by holding it, and you feel it.

Click 2: the lineage. Bricks (Fitzmaurice, Ishii, Buxton, 1995): graspable handles. Sensetable (Patten and Ishii, 2001): wireless pucks on a table. Actuated Workbench (Pangaro, Maynes-Aminzade, Ishii, 2002): the computer moves the puck.

Later, the table could also move the persona figurines of Tangible Co-ideation, the TMG project I contribute to.

Interaction Design is Humanism.
Sense-makingThinkingCreating

Key point: interaction design investigates the human condition and expands the life we experience by giving us new senses: for the body, for knowledge, and for connection.

Norbert Wiener (body): cybernetics. Machines and organisms share feedback loops, so tools extend the body.

J.C.R. Licklider (knowledge): man-computer symbiosis. Humans and computers thinking together, in real time.

Hiroshi Ishii (connection): tangible bits and radical atoms. Give digital information a physical form you can hold and share.

Can technology put humans in front of the decision, in the real world, and not behind a phone screen?

Click 1: the names become body, knowledge, connection. Click 2: Interaction Design is Humanism. Click 3: the keywords sense-making, thinking, creating.

Land it: this thesis extends our senses toward thinking, sense making, and creating. I think that is worth pursuing.
