Interview Q1: Motivation
Why create the "$1 Recognizer"?

UI prototypers rarely have pattern-matching toolkits. $1 uses pure geometry in ~100 lines of code, needs no training sets, and hits 97%+ accuracy with 1 template.

Comparison vs. Rubine & DTW
  • Rubine's Classifier: Needs many training examples, matrix inversions, and Mahalanobis distances (92.83% accuracy in study).
  • Dynamic Time Warping (DTW): Accurate (99.15%), but O(N2) matching took 128.26 mins per test set vs. 1.59 mins for $1 (99.02% accuracy).
Interview Q2: Rotation Invariance & GSS
Why does GSS guarantee 10 iterations?

Rotating indicative angle ω to 0° gets within ~4.2° of optimal. Bounded GSS then searches [−45°,+45°] down to a 2° threshold in 10 fixed evaluations.

Mathematical Proof of 10 Evaluations

Hill-Climbing takes 53.5 rotations on dissimilar gestures. GSS narrows the 90° bracket by φ=12(5−1)≈0.618 each step:

90°×φ7=3.10°>2°⟹ 90°×φ8=1.92°≤2°

2 initial interior evaluations + 8 loop reductions (1 new evaluation per step) = 10 iterations, saving 80.2% work.

Interview Q3: Limitations & Human Factors
What are the trade-offs & speed findings?

Because $1 rotates to 0° and scales non-uniformly to a square, it cannot distinguish squares from rectangles, circles from ovals, or orientation-specific arrows.

Articulation Speed & 1D Lines
  • Medium Speed Wins: Across 4,800 gestures, medium speed (~1.15s) had fewer errors (2.46%) than slow/tentative (2.84%) or fast/sloppy (3.22%) strokes.
  • 1D Lines: Non-uniform scaling distorts thin straight lines; check bounding-box aspect ratio to scale 1D gestures uniformly.
