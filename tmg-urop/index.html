About Tangible Media Group & Tangible AI

The Tangible Media Group (TMG) at the MIT Media Lab, led by Professor Hiroshi Ishii, has spent over 30 years researching human-computer interaction by bridging the digital and physical worlds.

Our Tangible AI effort focuses on giving intelligence a physical, interactive body. Instead of interacting with AI solely through text prompts in isolated chat windows, we build physical interfaces, custom hardware, real-time multimodal interaction systems, and domain-specific languages that fuse AI capabilities directly into physical environments and end-user programming workflows.

What you will work on

Your day-to-day work spans design and storytelling, physical fabrication, software engineering, machine learning, and publication:

  • Design & storytelling: Collaborative brainstorming, whiteboarding, concept ideation, and crafting compelling visual/interaction narratives.
  • Computer vision & multimodal models: Training and fine-tuning vision models for spatial tracking and object recognition.
  • Real-time interaction systems: Engineering low-latency speech, gesture, vision, and robotic interaction loops for human-computer collaboration.
  • Domain-specific languages (DSLs): Designing DSLs and programming abstractions for end-user behavioral programming and direct manipulation.
  • Hardware & embedded systems: Fabricating custom hardware, embedded circuits, and tangible physical objects.
  • Impact & dissemination: Shipping impactful work either as top-tier conference publications or open-source software projects.

Who this is for

Good fit

  • You enjoy the ideas behind Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs (SICP) and thinking deeply about programming abstractions.
  • You have designed, CAD modeled, and 3D printed physical objects or mechanical assemblies.
  • You enjoy creating art with code or building interactive media.
  • You ask why to build something, more than just how or what to build.

Poor fit

  • You rely primarily on tokenmaxxing or offloading all problem-solving to AI output without understanding the underlying mechanics.
  • You are oversubscribed with classes or other commitments and looking for a low-effort credit filler.
  • You are only interested in theoretical machine learning research without building working physical or software artifacts.

What to submit when applying

When reaching out, please include your resume/portfolio and answers to the following four questions:

  1. Goals: What skills do you want to build during this UROP, and what goals are you pursuing that this project supports?
  2. Projects: Describe something cool you have made (hardware, software, art, or design).
  3. Reflections on failure: Share a mistake or technical failure from a past project. What happened, and what would you do differently now?
  4. AI usage: How do you currently use AI tools in your workflow? What works well, and what do you find frustrating or limiting?
