Tangible AI: UROP Proposal Writing Guide

Use this guide to draft your formal Fall 2026 MIT UROP research proposal for the Tangible AI initiative.

A strong proposal demonstrates clear technical intent, realistic scope, and genuine ownership. It must fulfill every official MIT UROP standard while reflecting the Tangible Media Group (TMG) ethos: building to think, taking creative initiative, and learning in public.

Header

Name: [Your Full Name]
Faculty Supervisor: Hiroshi Ishii
Direct Supervisor: Chuanqi Sun
Term: Fall 2026
Date: [Submission Date]

[Project Title: Choose a concise, descriptive title reflecting your focus]

Project Overview

Provide the background, aim, and scope of your proposed research, naming your lab and collaborators.

Ground your overview in the core mission of Tangible AI: closing the gap between real-time direct manipulation and modern AI systems. Explain why screen-bound interfaces, text prompting, and chat boxes limit human expressiveness, and define the interaction bottleneck your chosen work addresses.

Explicitly state that you are conducting research within the Tangible Media Group at the MIT Media Lab, working under the guidance of Professor Hiroshi Ishii and direct supervisor Chuanqi Sun alongside a peer cohort. Introduce the specific projects from the Tangible AI project list that you plan to tackle.

Projects list: https://code.chuanqisun.com/tmg-urop/projects

Questions to guide your draft:

  • What physical or spatial interaction paradigm are you exploring to replace passive chat interfaces?
  • Can you explain the core interaction loop to an engineer or designer in two clear sentences?

Communication with Mentor(s)

State your exact meeting cadence, working locations, and communication channels.

Use and adapt the following baseline structure:

  • You will attend a weekly 1-hour in-person stand-up meeting in the Tangible Media Group space at the Media Lab for status updates, design critique, and task coordination.
  • You will participate in breakout work sessions with your direct supervisor and peer UROP cohort immediately following the stand-up.
  • You will maintain ongoing communication between meetings via Slack and ad hoc Zoom syncs to resolve technical blockers, share working builds, and coordinate code or hardware integration.

Research Role & Tentative Work Plan

Detail what you will personally contribute, your week-by-week execution roadmap, and your final deliverables.

Structure your semester around a staged workload: choose one small (S) project from the list as a rapid ramp-up build, followed by one or two medium/large (M/L) projects as your primary research objective.

Personal Research Role

Clarify your core technical focus. Are you leading hardware fabrication, computer vision tracking, low-latency audio/visual pipelines, interaction mechanics, or frontend systems engineering? Define where your direct contribution sits within the overall system.

Responsibilities and Duties

Break your semester down into concrete, progressive phases:

  • Weeks 1 to 3 (Ramp-up): Specify the small starter project you will prototype, the environment or bench tests you will set up, and the initial baseline you will establish.
  • Weeks 4 to 8 (Core Implementation): Detail your work on the primary project. List the core systems, physical assemblies, or interaction loops you will design and build.
  • Weeks 9 to 12 (Iteration and Testing): Describe how you will evaluate latency, refine ergonomics, troubleshoot failure points, and stress-test the prototype.
  • Weeks 13 to end (Synthesis and Documentation): Outline how you will package clean code, write clear documentation, and prepare demo media.

Anticipated Deliverables

List concrete, verifiable artifacts you will produce by the end of the term:

  • A functional, tested physical or software prototype that demonstrates real-time interaction.
  • Documented open-source repositories with clean architectures and clear reproduction instructions.
  • Short video documentation demonstrating the live user interaction loop.
  • A final project write-up or technical note suitable for lab dissemination.

Personal Statement & Goals

Explain your motivation, mutual technical growth, and personal definitions of success for the term.

Why You Are Interested

Reflect on your personal stance on tools and technology. Why does physical, sensory, or embodied interaction matter to you? Why do you want to build at the Media Lab instead of pursuing standard software development or purely theoretical machine learning?

What You Hope to Gain and What You Can Teach

Research on this team is a mutual exchange:

  • What you want to learn: Identify explicit technical and design gaps you plan to close, whether that involves embedded circuits, real-time multimodal models, CAD, or system architecture.
  • What you can teach: Name specific skills, previous build experiences, or engineering habits you can actively share with your peers during lab sessions.

Personal Goals Throughout the Term

Define clear metrics of personal success. Consider what habits of technical independence, handling ambiguous design problems, and shipping working artifacts you want to solidify by the end of Fall 2026.

Final checklist

  • Typo and grammar check. AI tools can help.
  • No generic/unrealisitc statements about technology changing the world.
  • A specific plan showing how you will build, test, and ship your ideas. (The plan will change, that's ok)
  • The timeline is realistic for your weekly commitment
  • Be yourself
