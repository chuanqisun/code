# Hold That Thought: UROP Proposal Writing Guide

Use this guide to draft your formal IAP 2027 MIT UROP research proposal for the Hold That Thought project.

A strong proposal demonstrates clear research intent, realistic scope, and genuine ownership. It must fulfill every official MIT UROP standard while reflecting the Tangible Media Group (TMG) ethos: building to think, taking creative initiative, and learning in public.

Write your proposal directly into the sections below.

Fill out this required header block at the very top of your document:

## Project Overview

Provide the background, aim, and scope of your proposed research, naming your lab and collaborators.

Ground your overview in the core problem: today, people use AI by taking turns. You prompt, wait, read the result, and prompt again. You can't grab an idea and nudge it while you watch it change. This project gives handles to ideas: simple controls bound to the meaning of what an LLM or image model generates, such as a slider bound to "more poetic" or one puck per idea in a brainstorm. Handles can be on screen or physical. Either way, the output changes in real time while you move the handle, with no submit button. The research question is whether people working this way are more creative than with chat, feel more in control, and enjoy it more.

Explicitly state that you are conducting research within the Tangible Media Group at the MIT Media Lab, working under the guidance of Professor Hiroshi Ishii and direct supervisor Chuanqi Sun alongside a peer cohort, and that your work feeds into Chuanqi's master's thesis.

Watch the thesis proposal presentation to catch up on context and challenges: https://youtu.be/letrre__TD4?t=151

### Choose your project

Use these example projects for inspiration.

1. **Verb Palette:** a text editor where semantic verbs (steer, blend, scale, bind) are tools, distilled from about 30 meaning-editing systems.
2. **Semantic Dial:** LLM-suggested axes (cautious to bold, concrete to abstract) as sliders that rewrite text instantly as you drag.
3. **Idea Tokens:** draggable idea cards, on screen or on a table, that blend by proximity and constrain by stacking.
4. **Material Handles:** semantic sliders with physical behavior, snapping to strong variants and getting sticky near clichés.
5. **Where Real Time Breaks:** a sketch-to-sentence toy with adjustable delay, to find where shaping turns into asking.

Propose your own idea that contributes to the core vision of the "Hold That Thought" thesis: how will you should tie meaning to a handle and go through all three stages: design, build, and evaluate?

### Anchor it in one concrete scenario

Abstract visions are hard to believe. Before you describe the system, describe one person using it: what they want, what they physically do, what the AI does in response, and what they walk away with. Walk through the full loop, from a real need to a meaningful outcome. A complete, specific scenario is more convincing than a broad list of possible applications.

**Questions to guide your draft:**

- What is the handle in your project, and what meaning is it bound to?
- Can you explain the core interaction loop to an engineer or designer in two clear sentences?
- Who is your user, and what are they trying to do in your scenario?
- **Affordance check:** If someone sees your handle for the first time, how do they know what to do with it? What shape, label, motion, or feedback tells them? A blank puck or an unlabeled slider communicates nothing. Design for this explicitly.

## Communication with Mentor(s)

State your exact meeting cadence, working locations, and communication channels.

Use and adapt the following baseline structure:

- You will attend a weekly 1-hour in-person stand-up meeting in the Tangible Media Group space at the Media Lab for status updates, design critique, and task coordination.
- You will participate in breakout work sessions with your direct supervisor and peer UROP cohort immediately following the stand-up.
- You will maintain ongoing communication between meetings via Slack and ad hoc Zoom syncs to resolve blockers, share working demos, and coordinate with peers.

You plan your own work. Your mentor provides a question, an end point, and a weekly meeting. Deciding what to try next and what to drop is up to you, so your proposal should show how you will make those calls.

## Research Role & Tentative Work Plan

Detail what you will personally contribute, your week-by-week roadmap, and your final deliverables.

Let AI do the programming. Most of your time should go to design and research: reading papers, prototyping ideas, designing and running a study, analyzing the data, and writing it up.

The example projects are sized for 1 to 3 months, and IAP is four weeks. Scope down rather than up: pick one project and cut it to a version you can design, build, and evaluate in IAP. A narrow study that fully runs beats a broad vision that half works. Put the bigger ideas in a "future work" paragraph.

### Personal Research Role

Clarify your focus. Are you leading the literature synthesis, the interaction design, the study design and analysis, or the physical hardware (for physical projects)? Define where your direct contribution sits within the overall project.

### Responsibilities and Duties

IAP runs four weeks, from Monday, January 4 to Friday, January 29, 2027. Break your term down into the three stages:

- **Week 1, Jan 4 to 8 (Design):** Read the related papers, sketch your design, and try early versions on a few people. Draft your study protocol.
- **Weeks 2 to 3, Jan 11 to 22 (Build):** Build the prototype so your scenario runs end to end in real time, and pilot your study. Note that Monday, Jan 18 is the MLK Day holiday.
- **Week 4, Jan 25 to 29 (Evaluate and Write):** Run the study, analyze the data, and write it up. Everything should be shipped by Friday, Jan 29.

### Anticipated Deliverables

List concrete, verifiable artifacts you will produce by the end of the term:

- A trail of working demos, ending in a prototype that runs your concrete scenario in real time.
- Study data with your analysis.
- A short paper draft covering design, build, and evaluation.
- Short video documentation demonstrating the live interaction loop.

## Personal Statement & Goals

Explain your motivation, mutual growth, and personal definitions of success for the term.

### Why You Are Interested

Reflect on your personal stance on tools and technology. Why does shaping ideas directly, rather than prompting, matter to you? Are you a tool maker, not just a consumer? If you have made a tool before, show it.

### What You Hope to Gain and What You Can Teach

Research on this team is a mutual exchange:

- **What you want to learn:** Identify the research and design skills you plan to build, such as study design, statistics, qualitative analysis, or academic writing.
- **What you can teach:** Name specific skills, previous build experiences, or habits you can actively share with your peers during lab sessions.

### Personal Goals Throughout the Term

Define clear metrics of personal success. Consider what habits of independent planning, handling ambiguous design problems, and shipping working artifacts you want to solidify by the end of IAP.
